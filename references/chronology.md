# Chronology Notes

This document records decisions about the internal ordering of material drawn from the lifetime narrative.

The goal is transparency about sequence choices without introducing post-crucifixion frameworks or later interpretive structures.

The main book is organized thematically (see `supporting/methodology.md`), not as a continuous chronological narrative. The notes below document the principal ordering decisions and the lifetime-record basis for them.

---

## Overall Organization Decision

**Decision:** Chapters are arranged thematically rather than in strict sequential order of events.

**Rationale (lifetime record only):** The Gospel accounts themselves present overlapping and non-identical sequences of the same lifetime events and sayings. A thematic arrangement allows related material (core announcement, authority claims, ethical demands, parables, encounters, discipleship requirements, conflicts, and final-period actions) to be gathered for clarity while remaining strictly inside the pre-crucifixion boundary. No attempt is made to construct a single “correct” absolute chronology beyond what the lifetime narratives themselves support.

---

## Placement of Core Proclamation First

**Decision:** The initial announcement of the kingdom’s nearness and the call to repent and believe is placed in Chapter 1.

**Rationale:** The lifetime narratives locate this announcement at the opening of Jesus’ public activity in Galilee after John’s arrest (Mark 1:14–15 and parallels). It functions as the stated purpose of his preaching (Luke 4:43) and is the message he entrusts to those he sends out. Placing it first reflects its foundational position in the lifetime record without claiming a later theological development.

---

## Authority, Ethical Teaching, and Parables as Mid-Ministry Clusters

**Decision:** Chapters 2–4 gather material associated primarily with the Galilean and itinerant periods of activity.

**Rationale:** Claims of authority (teaching not as the scribes, forgiveness of sins, lordship of the Sabbath, relation to the Father, antitheses), ethical instruction (Beatitudes, love of enemies, treasure and anxiety, hearing and doing, greatest commandments), and the kingdom parables are presented in the lifetime accounts as occurring during the period of public teaching and confrontation in Galilee and surrounding regions, prior to the final ascent to Jerusalem. Thematic grouping preserves these clusters without forcing a day-by-day sequence that the sources do not uniformly supply.

---

## Encounters and Discipleship

**Decision:** Chapters 5 and 6 collect individual and group interactions and the demands of following, drawn from across the lifetime ministry.

**Rationale:** The lifetime record contains both early calls (Simon, Andrew, James, John, Matthew/Levi) and later individual approaches (centurion, Canaanite woman, Zacchaeus, Bartimaeus, rich young man, etc.). Discipleship sayings (self-denial, counting the cost, priority over family, service) appear in multiple settings. Grouping by theme keeps the focus on the recorded interactions and demands while remaining inside the lifetime boundary; no post-crucifixion commissioning material is used.

---

## Conflict Material

**Decision:** Chapter 7 gathers controversies and confrontations.

**Rationale:** The lifetime narratives record opposition beginning early (Nazareth rejection, Beelzebul accusation, tradition-of-the-elders dispute) and intensifying in Jerusalem (authority challenge, tax question, Sadducees, woes). Thematic collection of these lifetime confrontations does not require resolving every chronological difference among the accounts.

---

## Final Days as the Terminal Chapter

**Decision:** Chapter 8 is restricted to the final period of public activity in and around Jerusalem and ends at the delivery of Jesus to be crucified.

**Rationale (lifetime record only):** The Gospel accounts locate the entry into Jerusalem, temple cleansing, Mount of Olives discourse, anointing at Bethany, last meal, Gethsemane prayer, arrest, and trials before the high priest and Pilate as the concluding sequence of events while Jesus is still alive. The project cutoff is explicit: material narrated as occurring after the crucifixion is excluded. Predictions spoken while alive (including references to future raising or coming) remain eligible because they are words uttered inside the lifetime; the narrative of events that follow the death is not. Ending the chapter at Pilate’s delivery to crucifixion keeps the entire book strictly pre-crucifixion.

---

## Parallel Accounts and Variation

**Decision:** Where parallel lifetime accounts differ in wording or detail, the compilation presents the material thematically or selects a clear rendering without adjudicating a single “historical” sequence on theological grounds.

**Rationale:** The methodology requires fidelity to the lifetime setting. Differences among the pre-crucifixion portions of the four accounts are left unresolved by later tradition; the passage map records the source locations so that every included item remains traceable.

---

## Summary of Sequence Logic

1. Foundational announcement (early Galilean proclamation).
2. Demonstrations and claims of authority, ethical demands, and kingdom illustrations (public teaching period).
3. Recorded encounters with individuals and groups, together with the cost of discipleship.
4. Controversies with religious authorities.
5. Final Jerusalem-period actions, teaching, meal, prayer, arrest, and trials — stopping before the crucifixion itself.

All decisions above are derived solely from the placement of material inside the lifetime narratives. No post-crucifixion framework, Pauline chronology, or later ecclesiastical calendar is employed.
